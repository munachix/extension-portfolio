How it works

Three steps, then it just runs.

  1. Pick your countries

    US, UK, Canada, the Netherlands, or all four. The lists download once and update weekly.

  2. Browse jobs as usual

    Badges appear next to the company name on job pages and on search results.

  3. Click a badge for details

    The official registered name, visa routes and rating, and for the US, how many H-1B filings.

The details

Built for the way it really goes.

Official sources only

US Department of Labor LCA disclosures, the GOV.UK register of licensed sponsors, Canada’s positive LMIA list and the Dutch IND recognised sponsors.

Handles messy names

“Google” on LinkedIn finds “Google (UK) Limited”; “JPMorganChase” finds “JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.”. Near matches say “likely” and show the registered name so you can judge.

Search any company

Type a name in the panel to check a company before you even find a job there.

Honest about limits

Being licensed to sponsor doesn’t mean a company will sponsor a particular job. VisaFlag tells you where to look, not what an employer will do.

Questions

Things people ask first.

How current is the data?

The UK, Dutch and Canadian lists are refreshed weekly from the government sites. US H-1B data is published quarterly by the Department of Labor and updated when it is.

Does “Sponsor” mean they’ll sponsor me?

No. It means the company is on the official register or has filed for visas recently. Always confirm with the employer. This isn’t legal advice.

Does VisaFlag see what I browse?

It reads the company name on the page you’re viewing and matches it on your computer. Your browsing is never sent anywhere.
